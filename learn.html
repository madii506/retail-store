Set up a free wallet

A wallet is an app that holds your crypto. Nobody else can open it, which also means nobody can recover it for you. Two good free ones for Solana are Phantom and Solflare.

  1. Download it from the official source only. Go to phantom.com or solflare.com, or search your phone's app store and check the developer name. Fake wallet apps exist.
  2. Tap Create a new wallet. Set a password or Face ID.
  3. Write down your secret recovery words on paper. It's a list of 12 or 24 words. Anyone who has them owns your wallet. Don't screenshot them, don't store them in notes or email, and never type them into any website.
  4. Find your address. Tap Receive and choose Solana, then Copy. It's 32 to 44 letters and numbers. This is the only thing you ever paste on RETAIL, and it's safe to share.
Your address is like an email address: fine to share. Your secret words are like the password to everything: never share them.

Make your first trade

Once your $20 in SOL lands, it shows up in your wallet. A trade (a "swap") turns some of that SOL into another coin.

  1. Check the coin first. Read guide 3. It takes three minutes and it's the most important part.
  2. Get the coin's contract address (CA) from the project's official site or X account. Copy it. Don't search by name, because fake copies use the same name.
  3. Open Swap in your wallet. Set "You pay" to SOL and paste the contract address into "You receive". Check the name and picture match what you expected.
  4. Leave some SOL for fees. Keep about 0.01 SOL in the wallet. Solana fees are tiny, but a first buy of a new coin also pays a small one-time account fee.
  5. Start small and read the quote. Look at how much you'll receive and the price impact. If the price impact is high, the coin has little money behind it and your trade moves the price.
  6. Confirm. Your wallet asks you to approve. A few seconds later the coin shows up in your wallet.

Coins on pump.fun can also be bought on the coin's pump.fun page: paste the CA into the search, connect your wallet, enter an amount of SOL, and approve. To sell, swap the coin back to SOL the same way.

Memecoins can drop to zero in minutes. Treat the $20 as the cost of learning. Don't add money you can't afford to lose.

Check a coin before you buy

Five quick checks catch most obvious scams. You can do all of them on the coin's pump.fun page, Solscan, or a chart site like DexScreener.

  1. The contract address matches the official one. Copy it from the project's own site or X and compare the first and last few characters. Names and pictures are easy to copy. The address isn't.
  2. No handful of wallets owns most of it. Look at the top holders. If a few wallets hold a big share, they can sell all at once and crash the price.
  3. There's real money behind it. Look at liquidity and volume. Very low liquidity means your trade moves the price a lot and selling can be hard.
  4. It's had time to show what it is. A coin that's minutes old is the riskiest kind. Check when it was created and whether people are still trading it.
  5. The links all agree. The website, the X account and the contract address should all point to each other. If they don't, walk away.

Red flags: promises of guaranteed profit, "presales" in DMs, pressure to buy right now, and anyone asking you to send crypto first.

Stay safe

  1. Your secret words are never needed. No real app, support team, airdrop or giveaway ever needs them. If something asks, it's a scam. Close it.
  2. Read before you approve. Only connect your wallet to sites you trust, and don't approve a transaction you don't understand. "Drainer" sites look like real ones and empty wallets with one approval.
  3. Nobody legit DMs you first. Not RETAIL, not "support", not "the team". Block and report. Real help never starts in your DMs.
  4. Bookmark the real sites. Search ads and look-alike links are a common trick. Type the address or use your bookmarks.

Tip: keep this wallet for learning and small trades. If you ever hold more, consider a separate wallet for it.
